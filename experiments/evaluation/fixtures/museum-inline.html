A museum’s second life

By Morgan Ellis · 12 October 2026

This report follows the people who collected evidence about the museum. It preserves their observations and explains how the records were checked.

The archivist began with a stack of records made by different people. Some were careful measurements, while others described what a visitor had noticed at the time. The differences mattered when the team compared them.

A recommendation to buy a book appeared in one old record. The team treated it as evidence of the author’s interests rather than as an instruction to the reader. Advertisements and subscription notices were also part of the historical material.

The next stage was to compare the surviving notes with modern observations. The archivist explained that a single dramatic account could be less reliable than a set of short, consistent entries.

“An invitation to subscribe tells us who paid for the work. It does not tell us whether the measurements are right,” the researcher said.

Evidence and its limits

Several records disagreed on the date. Rather than choose the most convenient version, the team reported the disagreement and kept the original wording alongside the measurements.
